The Mathematics Behind the KBS Method

Modular Arithmetic Explained

1. Why Does Dividing the Last 2 Digits by 7 Work?

There are 7 days in a week. A normal year has 365 days. Notice that:
365 = 52 × 7 + 1

This means each regular year pushes the calendar forward by exactly 1 day! Over $YY$ years, the calendar advances by $YY$ ordinary days. When calculating days of the week, multiples of 7 have no effect, so:
YY mod 7 = Remainder R

2. Why Does Dividing by 4 Count Leap Years?

Every 4th year is a leap year with 366 days, which adds an extra leap day (+1 extra day). In $YY$ years, the total number of leap days that occurred is:
Quotient Q = ⌊YY / 4⌋

By adding $R + Q$, Sri Bhoomaiah combines the regular 1-day shifts and the extra leap-day shifts!

3. Why Subtract 1 for January and February in a Leap Year?

This is the most elegant detail of Sri Bhoomaiah's formula! The term $\lfloor YY / 4 \rfloor$ counts the leap year as already having occurred right from January 1st of that year.

However, the extra leap day is 29 February. Before 29 February occurs (in January and early February), that extra day hasn't happened yet! Therefore, Sri Bhoomaiah compensates by subtracting 1 for January and February in a leap year.

4. Why Do the Month Codes Work?

The month codes represent the cumulative shift in days modulo 7 starting from January:

  • January: starts with code 0. (31 days ≡ 3 mod 7).
  • February: starts at $0 + 3 = 3$. (28 days in common year ≡ 0 mod 7).
  • March: starts at $3 + 0 = 3$. (31 days ≡ 3 mod 7).
  • April: starts at $3 + 3 = 6$. (30 days ≡ 2 mod 7).
  • May: starts at $6 + 2 = 1$ (since $8 \equiv 1 \pmod 7$).
  • June: starts at $1 + 31 = 4$ (since $32 \equiv 4 \pmod 7$).
  • July: starts at $4 + 30 = 6$ (since $34 \equiv 6 \pmod 7$).
  • August: starts at $6 + 31 = 2$ (since $37 \equiv 2 \pmod 7$).
  • September: starts at $2 + 31 = 5$ (since $33 \equiv 5 \pmod 7$).
  • October: starts at $5 + 30 = 0$ (since $35 \equiv 0 \pmod 7$).
  • November: starts at $0 + 31 = 3$ (since $31 \equiv 3 \pmod 7$).
  • December: starts at $3 + 30 = 5$ (since $33 \equiv 5 \pmod 7$).

5. Why Does the 2000s Century Need "Minus 1"?

In the 1900s, 1900 was not a leap year, leaving 24 leap years. Over 100 years:
365 × 100 + 24 = 36,524 days
36,524 mod 7 = 5 ≡ -2

Combined with the millennium leap year 2000, the offset between the 1900s and 2000s shifts by -1 (which is the same as +6 mod 7). That is why Sri Bhoomaiah correctly noted: to work for the century, simply subtract 1!
